The Season 2 Paradox

New seasons shrink, but they bring old ones back

I combined 7 Netflix engagement reports (2023–2026) with weekly Top 10 data, then used event studies, matching and conformal forecasting to turn a descriptive puzzle into renewal and release-strategy recommendations, written up as a one-page memo.

One clean table from seven messy reports

Netflix publishes engagement reports twice a year, and title names drift between them ("Season 2", "Limited Series", localized names). I wrote title-parsing rules that link every season of a show across reports.

The result is a 116K-row panel covering 32K titles, joined with weekly Top 10 rankings for the release-strategy questions.

A new season lifts the old ones by 173%

A stacked difference-in-differences event study shows that when a new season drops, viewing of earlier seasons jumps. For every 100 views of the new season, about 51 extra views go to earlier seasons.

Split releases stay in the Top 10 about 4 weeks longer

Releasing a season in two parts keeps a show on the chart longer than dropping it all at once. I estimated this with nearest-neighbor matching on comparable titles.

Enterprise Fraud Risk Scoring & Model Validation Framework

Catch the fraud, stop flagging good customers

An end-to-end fraud detection pipeline on synthetic payment data: 25 engineered features, XGBoost against a logistic regression baseline, a tuned decision threshold, and an automated validation suite that checks stability, drift, fairness and explanations before anything ships.

Nov 2024 · Python · XGBoost · scikit-learn · SHAP · KS test · PSI

25 features from 10K payments

The pipeline runs on 10K synthetic payment transactions with a 3% fraud rate, which is rare enough that accuracy alone would be misleading.

I engineered 25 features across spending velocity, amount anomalies, timing and behavior: z-scored amounts, country and device mismatches, and each merchant's fraud history.

87% fewer false alarms

Every false positive is a real customer whose payment gets blocked. XGBoost cut the false-positive rate from 16.9% to 2.2%.

I then tuned the decision threshold to balance precision and recall, instead of accepting the default cutoff of 0.5.
